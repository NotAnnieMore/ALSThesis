\section*{Abstract}
\label{sec:abstract}

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) is a fatal neurodegenerative disease with a median survival of two to four years and high variability in disease course. Predicting individual prognosis from baseline clinical data is clinically relevant for trial stratification, care planning, and therapeutic decision support, but requires careful handling of class imbalance, temporal data leakage, and model interpretability.

This dissertation presents two complementary studies, both using the PRO-ACT database and both addressing leakage-free validation, class imbalance, threshold selection, and SHAP-based explainability.

Study~I develops an original classification pipeline for predicting rapid versus slow ALSFRS-R functional progression at six-month and three-month horizons. Thirty-five baseline clinical features across five data blocks are extracted from 1,392 subjects. Seven classifiers are tuned via Optuna Bayesian optimisation (60 trials per configuration, PR-AUC objective) under 5-fold GroupKFold cross-validation with subject-level partition assignment. The final model, XGBoost with unbalanced class weighting, achieves a held-out test PR-AUC of 0.456 (95\% CI: [0.364, 0.568]) and detects 72 of 84 rapid progressors at the F1-optimal threshold ($t\!=\!0.21$), with recall 85.7\% and precision 35.1\%. SHAP analysis identifies baseline ALSFRS-R total (mean $|$SHAP$|\!=\!0.325$) and FVC in litres (0.131) as the dominant predictors.

Study~II replicates and extends the survival prognosis framework of Papaiz et al.\ (2024), which classifies ALS patients as short survivors (death within 24 months of symptom onset). From 1,502 subjects and 23 ordinal and binary features, seven classifiers are evaluated across ten resampling strategies (70 combinations) with Optuna optimisation (50 trials per combination, ROC-AUC objective) and a held-out test set of 301 subjects. LightGBM with random oversampling achieves the best test ROC-AUC of 0.923 (95\% CI: [0.868, 0.966]) and recall of 0.857 (30 of 35 short survivors detected). The multi-layer perceptron reaches test ROC-AUC of 0.918 but detects only 1 of 35 short survivors at the default threshold, demonstrating ROC-AUC--sensitivity decoupling under severe class imbalance (11.6\% minority). Threshold optimisation partially recovers recall (0.029 to 0.486) but does not eliminate the structural gap. BalancedBagging is confirmed to significantly improve balanced accuracy for six of seven classifiers (Wilcoxon signed-rank with Bonferroni correction, $p\!<\!0.01$). SHAP attribution identifies diagnosis delay (mean $|$SHAP$|\!=\!0.986$) as the dominant predictor.

Across both studies, the results show that metric choice, threshold selection, and validation design determine whether results are clinically interpretable, and that leakage-free, reproducible, and explainable machine learning for ALS prognosis is achievable with publicly available data.
