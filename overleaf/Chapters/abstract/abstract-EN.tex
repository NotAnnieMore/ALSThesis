\begin{center}
\textbf{Explainable and Reproducible Machine Learning for ALS Prognosis: Functional Progression Classification and Survival Prediction}
\end{center}

\section*{Abstract}
\label{sec:abstract}

Two PRO-ACT studies examined machine learning for amyotrophic lateral sclerosis prognosis. Study~I classified rapid functional progression at three and six months. At six months, XGBoost achieved test PR-AUC 0.456, detecting 72 of 84 rapid progressors with 85.7\% recall and 35.1\% precision. SHAP highlighted baseline ALSFRS-R and forced vital capacity. Study~II classified death within 24 months of symptom onset. LightGBM with random oversampling achieved test ROC-AUC 0.923 and detected 30 of 35 Short Survivors. BalancedBagging significantly improved balanced accuracy in four of five directly comparable reference-study families. Ranking performance did not determine threshold behaviour. Patient separation, outcome construction, imbalance handling, and threshold selection affected interpretation. Study~II's delayed entry and post-diagnosis predictors restrict its prognostic interpretation. External, prospective validation remains necessary.

\section*{Keywords}
\label{sec:keywords}

Amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, explainable machine learning, functional progression, survival prognosis, class imbalance, PRO-ACT.
