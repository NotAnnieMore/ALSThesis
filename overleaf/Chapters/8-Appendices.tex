\section{FVC Missingness Analysis}
\label{app:fvc_missingness}

This section reports the complete analysis of baseline \gls{FVC} availability summarised in Section~\ref{sec:results_fvc_missingness}. FVC in litres is missing for 43.7\% of the six-month cohort. Participants with and without an observed value are compared to determine whether availability is visibly associated with patient characteristics, trial attributes, or the target slope.

Eight continuous variables are examined: baseline \gls{ALSFRS-R} total, age, six-month slope, creatinine, \gls{ALT}, \gls{BMI}, weight, and concomitant-medication count. Sex, study arm, and documented riluzole use are treated as categorical variables. Continuous distributions are compared using two-sided Mann--Whitney~$U$ tests and absolute Cohen's~$d$ values based on the pooled standard deviation. Categorical associations use Pearson's $\chi^2$ test and Cram\'er's~$V$:
\[
  V=\sqrt{\frac{\chi^2}{n\min(r-1,c-1)}}.
\]

Raw $p$-values are reported alongside effect sizes. The family of 11 comparisons is interpreted using a Bonferroni threshold of $0.05/11=0.0045$. Table~\ref{tab:fvc_missingness} marks nominal $p<0.05$ separately so that nominal and multiplicity-adjusted results are not conflated.

\input{images/tables/step5v2_fvc_missingness.tex}
\break

Baseline \gls{ALSFRS-R} ($p=0.345$, $d=0.027$), age ($p=0.459$, $d=0.031$), and sex ($p=0.408$, $V=0.022$) show little observed difference between participants with and without FVC. The target slope gives $p=0.050$ and $d=0.098$. Mean slopes are $-0.91$ and $-1.00$ points per 30 days, a difference of 0.09 relative to an overall standard deviation of 0.89. Creatinine ($p=0.092$) and ALT ($p=0.097$) also do not meet the nominal threshold.

The largest categorical association is study arm ($V=0.357$, $p<0.001$), while concomitant-medication count differs with $d=0.332$ ($p<0.001$). These results are compatible with variation in measurement protocols or recording density across the pooled trials. Riluzole has a nominal association ($p=0.015$, $V=0.065$), but its effect size is small and the result does not survive the Bonferroni threshold.

Weight ($d=0.146$, $p=0.003$) and BMI ($d=0.468$, $p<0.001$) also differ between availability groups. Their collection may be related to FVC measurement, although this was not tested directly. Under the Bonferroni threshold, study arm, BMI, weight, and concomitant-medication count remain the clearest observed differences.

The pattern is compatible with missingness related partly to trial-level collection practices, but observed-data comparisons cannot identify the missing-data mechanism. They do not prove that FVC is \gls{MAR}, and an \gls{MNAR} component cannot be excluded. Median imputation is therefore a pragmatic preprocessing decision rather than a consequence of a confirmed missingness assumption.

\section{BMI Data-Quality Audit}
\label{app:bmi_correction}

This section documents the source-data issue that prompted the \gls{BMI} correction described in Section~\ref{sec:methods_bmi_correction}. The raw \gls{PRO-ACT} vital-sign table contains 147 height records labelled ``Centimeters'' with values between 14.5 and 19.3, which are not plausible adult heights. Multiplying these values by ten gives 145--193\,cm, so a misplaced decimal point is a plausible explanation, but the intended measurement cannot be confirmed from the available records.

The feature-extraction function \texttt{to\_cm} passed values carrying the ``Centimeters'' unit through unchanged. The anomalous heights therefore reached the baseline feature file and produced impossible BMI values. For example, combining a height of 14.5\,cm with a weight of approximately 95\,kg gives a BMI close to 4{,}500 when calculated as
\[
  \text{BMI} = \frac{\text{weight (kg)}}{[\text{height (m)}]^2}.
\]

After the measurement closest to $t_0$ was selected for each participant, 132 of the 147 anomalous height records remained in \texttt{features\_vitals\_t0.csv} ($N=4{,}438$). Heights outside $[120,220]$\,cm and weights outside $[30,200]$\,kg were set to \texttt{NaN} rather than rescaled. This affected 132 selected heights and 31 weights. BMI was then recalculated only for records with a valid height and weight, while missing values were left for median imputation within each model's preprocessing pipeline.

Following this correction, BMI ranges from 15.5 to 46.9 in the six-month cohort ($N=1{,}392$), which is compatible with reported distributions in \gls{ALS} clinical-trial populations~\cite{Anani2023}. All horizon-specific datasets used for tuning, ablation, and subsequent analyses were regenerated from the cleaned vital-sign features.

\section{Exploratory Analysis of Treatment Variables}
\label{app:treatment_eda}

This section provides the complete exploratory analysis of the three treatment-related features used in Study~I: clinical trial arm, documented pre-baseline riluzole use, and the number of concomitant medications. The analysis uses all 1{,}392 participants in the six-month cohort and a descriptive rapid-progression label based on the global 30th-percentile slope cutoff (418 participants, 30.0\%). Model development does not use this global cutoff.

\subsection{Analysis Design}
\label{app:treatment_design}

Active and Placebo participants are compared using a $\chi^2$ test for the binary progression label and a two-sided Mann--Whitney~$U$ test for the continuous slope. Participants without study-arm information remain in the descriptive summary but are excluded from these tests. The same comparisons are performed for participants with and without documented pre-baseline riluzole use. Study arm and riluzole are also cross-tabulated, although no formal interaction test is conducted.

Concomitant-medication counts are grouped as 0, 1--2, 3--5, 6--9, and $\geq10$. The 15 most frequently recorded medications are identified from records beginning no later than each participant's $t_0$. Their progression rates are descriptive: no drug-level hypothesis tests or multiple-comparison claims are made.

The pooled data do not support causal interpretation. ``Active'' combines experimental arms from different trials rather than one intervention, riluzole exposure is observational, and medication burden may also reflect comorbidity, symptom management, trial procedures, or recording intensity.

\subsection{Concomitant Medications}
\label{subsec:results_eda_conmeds}

Participants with 1--2 pre-baseline concomitant medications have the lowest rapid-progression rate ($22.8$\%, $n\!=\!149$), while those with 6--9 have the highest ($34.2$\%, $n\!=\!310$). Medication count may capture comorbidity, symptom management, trial procedures, or record density, and the grouped rates do not separate these explanations.

The 15 most frequently recorded medications are identified from \texttt{PROACT\_CONMEDS} using participant-specific baseline dates (Figure~\ref{fig:eda_top_meds}). Riluzole is the most common entry (673 participants, 48.3\%), followed by Vitamin~E (355, 25.5\%) and ascorbic acid (311, 22.3\%). Rapid-progression rates range from 25.4\% for Betacarotene to 37.3\% for Colecalciferol. The raw concomitant-medication entry for riluzole is distinct from the dedicated riluzole-use feature analysed above.

\section{Hyperparameter Optimisation Details}

\subsection{Search Spaces and Tuning Diagnostics}
\label{app:tuning_diagnostics}

\input{images/tables/optuna_search_spaces.tex}

The 13 model--mode searches comprised 780 Optuna trials and 3,900 cross-validation fits. All trials completed successfully. The common budget of 60 trials was a computational choice rather than a convergence criterion: nine searches, including both XGBoost modes, found their final best value between trials 41 and 60. The histories therefore give no empirical basis for reducing the budget to 40 trials.

The recorded optimisation time was approximately 34 minutes on one machine. Individual searches ranged from 8 seconds for the Decision Tree to 768 seconds for the unbalanced SVM. Trial histories were stored as \gls{CSV} files, while selected parameters and summary metrics were saved as \gls{JSON} files for the subsequent analyses.

The search spaces ranged from two parameters for Logistic Regression to nine for LightGBM and XGBoost. One LightGBM dimension was ineffective: \texttt{subsample} was sampled, but row subsampling was disabled because \texttt{subsample\_freq} retained its default value of zero. The stored values are preserved for reproducibility but should not be read as an optimised source of variation.

Figure~\ref{fig:metric_heatmap} places the four recorded metrics side by side for all 13 selected configurations. It makes visible the weak correspondence between the ranking metrics and $F_1$/$F_2$ at the fixed threshold of 0.5 discussed in Section~\ref{subsec:results_metric_correlation}.

\section{Decision Curve Analysis}
\label{app:dca}

This section documents the Decision Curve Analysis summarised in Section~\ref{subsec:results_dca}. At threshold $t$, net benefit is defined as
\begin{equation}
  \text{NB}(t) = \frac{\text{TP}}{N} - \frac{\text{FP}}{N} \cdot \frac{t}{1-t},
  \label{eq:net_benefit}
\end{equation}
where $t/(1-t)$ represents the threshold-implied relative cost of a false positive compared with a missed rapid progressor.

The model is compared with two reference strategies: \textit{treat all}, which classifies every participant as rapid, and \textit{treat none}, which classifies none as rapid. Net benefit is evaluated from $t=0.01$ to $0.60$ in increments of 0.01. No participant has a predicted probability above 0.58, so higher thresholds reproduce the treat-none strategy and add no information.

\begin{figure}[ht!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{images/figures/decision_curve.pdf}
  \caption[Decision Curve Analysis, TEST set]{Decision Curve Analysis for XGBoost on the TEST set. The shaded region marks the main continuous interval ($t=0.18$--$0.47$) in which the model has higher estimated net benefit than both reference strategies.}
  \label{fig:decision_curve}
\end{figure}

The main continuous interval in which the model exceeds both references is $t\in[0.18,\,0.47]$. Higher net benefit also occurs at the isolated grid points 0.13, 0.54, and 0.57. These points are not treated as stable intervals, particularly at 0.54 and 0.57, where only three and one participants are classified as positive.

At the selected threshold of 0.21, the model's net benefit is 0.131, compared with 0.115 for treat all and 0 for treat none. The difference of 0.016 from treat all corresponds to approximately 1.6 additional net true-positive decisions per 100 participants under the weighting implied by this threshold.

These estimates come from the same finite TEST set used for model evaluation and are reported without confidence intervals. Moreover, the reference terminology does not correspond to a specified clinical intervention in this study. The curve can therefore identify thresholds with favourable estimated net benefit in this sample, but it cannot establish clinical utility or transportability to another cohort.

\section{Training-Size Sensitivity}
\label{app:learning_curve}

This section documents the learning-curve analysis summarised in Section~\ref{subsec:results_learning_curves}. The selected XGBoost configuration is fitted on progressively larger subsets of \gls{DEV}: 20\%, 30\%, and subsequent ten-percentage-point increments up to 100\%. Since DEV contains one row per participant, sampling is performed directly at participant level.

Each fraction below 100\% is evaluated across 20 stratified random samples, preserving the rapid-progressor proportion at approximately 30\%. The full DEV fit is deterministic and is run once. Figure~\ref{fig:learning_curve} reports mean \gls{PR-AUC} and \gls{ROC-AUC} across the repeated subsets, with error bars showing one standard deviation rather than confidence intervals.

\begin{figure}[ht!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{images/figures/learning_curve.pdf}
  \caption[XGBoost training-size sensitivity]{TEST-set performance after fitting XGBoost on progressively larger stratified subsets of DEV. Points below 100\% show the mean across 20 repetitions; error bars represent one standard deviation. The full DEV model is fitted once.}
  \label{fig:learning_curve}
\end{figure}

Mean PR-AUC is 0.424 at 20\% of DEV ($n=222$), 0.440 at 60\% ($n=667$), and 0.446 at 90\% ($n=1{,}001$). The corresponding standard deviation falls from 0.029 at 20\% to 0.011 at 90\%. Fitting the model once on all 1{,}113 DEV participants gives a TEST PR-AUC of 0.456.

The mean curve is not monotonic, and error bars overlap across most adjacent fractions. The observed pattern is therefore compatible with modest average gains and reduced variability as the training subset grows, but it does not identify a stable plateau. The full-data point has no repetition-based error bar and is not directly comparable with the variability estimated for the smaller fractions.

The same TEST set is evaluated at every fraction, making this a post-evaluation sensitivity analysis rather than a new estimate of generalisation performance. It was not used to select the model or its hyperparameters, and it cannot determine how much performance would change in a larger independent cohort.

\section{Partial Dependence Analysis}
\label{app:pdp}

This section documents the Partial Dependence Plots (PDPs) summarised in Section~\ref{subsec:results_pdp}. For a feature $x_j$, the partial dependence function is
\begin{equation}
  \hat{f}_j(x_j) = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \hat{f}(x_j,\, \mathbf{x}_{i,\setminus j}),
  \label{eq:pdp}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{x}_{i,\setminus j}$ contains the values of all features other than $x_j$ for participant $i$.

PDPs are computed for the three features with the highest global \gls{SHAP} importance: baseline \gls{ALSFRS-R} total, \gls{FVC} in litres, and age. For each feature, the evaluation grid spans the 5th to 95th percentile of its observed \gls{TEST} values. Missing values are excluded when defining the grid and rug marks but remain median-imputed within the prediction pipeline. The rug marks show where observed values are concentrated and help identify sparsely supported regions of each curve.

\begin{figure}[ht!]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{images/figures/pdp_top3.pdf}
  \caption[Partial Dependence Plots for top-3 SHAP features]{Partial dependence plots for the three highest-ranked global SHAP features: baseline ALSFRS-R total, FVC in litres, and age. Grids span the 5th--95th percentiles of observed TEST values; rug marks show observed data density.}
  \label{fig:pdp_top3}
\end{figure}
\newpage

For baseline \gls{ALSFRS-R}, the average predicted probability generally decreases across the observed range of 29--46, with several step changes characteristic of a tree ensemble. The total PDP range is 0.201. This direction is consistent with the global SHAP analysis, where higher baseline functional scores generally shift predictions towards the slow-progression class.

The \gls{FVC} curve is relatively flat below approximately 3.7 litres, falls around 3.8--4.0 litres, and then stabilises, producing a total PDP range of 0.080. Only 162 of 279 TEST participants have an observed FVC value in litres, so the rug plot is important when judging which regions have direct data support. For age, the average prediction rises around the low 40s and remains broadly stable across much of the higher range. Its total PDP range is 0.104, with age observed for 246 participants.

PDPs average over the joint distribution of all remaining variables. When predictors are correlated, replacing one feature across its grid can create combinations that are uncommon or clinically implausible in the observed cohort. The curves therefore describe the fitted model's marginal behaviour; they do not represent causal dose--response relationships or effects expected for an individual participant.

\chapter{Patient Risk Report Demonstrator}
\label{app:patient_report}
% ============================================================

This appendix documents the patient-level reporting demonstrator introduced in Section~\ref{sec:methods_patient_report}. It was developed after model evaluation to test how the persisted Study~I pipeline could present an individual prediction, its local explanation, and supporting context. The demonstrator is not a clinically validated decision-support tool.

\section{Report Design}
\label{app:patient_report_design}

The tool loads the XGBoost pipeline refitted on the complete eligible cohort (Section~\ref{sec:methods_final_model}) together with its feature list, slope cutoff, and decision threshold. It accepts input from three sources:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Dataset lookup:} a subject \gls{ID} from the processed cohort;
  \item \textbf{\gls{JSON} file:} a key--value file containing baseline feature values, which can represent an external or fictitious profile;
  \item \textbf{Demo mode:} a randomly selected record from the processed cohort.
\end{enumerate}

Each report contains three components. First, the model output gives the estimated probability of rapid progression, $P(\text{rapid})$, and the classification obtained at the preselected operating threshold ($t=0.21$). For cohort records, the retrospectively derived outcome is displayed for context; it is not a held-out comparison because those records contributed to the final refit. Second, a local TreeSHAP explanation lists the five features with the largest absolute Shapley values, including their observed values and direction of influence. Contributions from one-hot encoded columns are aggregated under the source variable. Third, descriptive rules flag whether any of 13 variables appearing among the five main contributors lies beyond a predefined 25th- or 75th-percentile cutoff derived from \gls{DEV}.

The percentile flags are distributional context rather than clinical reference ranges, alerts, or treatment recommendations. The report therefore includes a research-use disclaimer, and neither its predicted probabilities nor its review flags have undergone prospective clinical validation.

\section{Fictitious Profile Generator}
\label{subsec:methods_random_patient}

A companion script generates fictitious profiles containing all 35 baseline features, allowing the interface to be tested without selecting a named cohort record. Continuous variables are sampled from bounded distributions calibrated to approximate summaries from the full \gls{PRO-ACT} cohort ($N=1{,}392$), while categorical variables follow approximate observed frequencies. Two dependencies are imposed explicitly: \gls{BMI} is calculated from sampled weight and height, and \gls{FVC} in litres is conditioned on sex.

The remaining variables are sampled separately, so the profiles do not reproduce the clinical data's joint distribution. Their purpose is limited to testing data ingestion, prediction, and report formatting; they are unsuitable for clinical simulation, privacy assessment, or model validation.
